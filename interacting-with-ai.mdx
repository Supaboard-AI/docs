title: "Write Effective AI Prompts"
mode: "wide"
---

Most bad AI answers trace back to a prompt that left something open: no timeframe, no definition of the metric, or three questions bundled into one. The agent fills those gaps with guesses, and its guess may not match what you meant.

The fix is mechanical. A prompt that works pins down:

| Component | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Timeframe | Defines the analysis period |
| Metric | Specifies exactly what should be calculated |
| Totals | Requests the supporting numbers that let you check the answer |
| Context | Explains the business intent |
| Segmentation | Defines grouping, if you need any |

Say *when*, say *what to calculate*, and ask for *the totals behind the result*. That covers most of it. Things to avoid are the mirror image: no timeframe, several KPIs in one prompt, undefined metrics, subjective wording ("how well", "why is it high").

# Before and after

The examples below are drawn from real failure patterns. In each one the vague version isn't wrong, exactly — it's underspecified, and the answer suffers in a predictable way.

## Counting a problem instead of costing it

<Prompt description="Show the number of failed transactions this month." icon="user-robot-xmarks" actions={["copy"]}>
Show the number of failed transactions this month.
</Prompt>

You'll get a count. But if what you actually care about is the money, the count doesn't tell you — and "this month" may be interpreted as calendar month or last 30 days.

<Prompt description="For March, calculate the total value of failed transactions. Include both the number of failed transactions and the total value affected." icon="user-robot-xmarks" actions={["copy"]}>
For March, calculate the total value of failed transactions. Include both the number of failed transactions and the total value affected.
</Prompt>

## Comparisons with no anchor

<Prompt description="How did this month perform compared to last month?" icon="user-robot-xmarks" actions={["copy"]}>
How did this month perform compared to last month?
</Prompt>

"Perform" on what metric? Which months, exactly? Name the metric and the months, and ask for the difference to be explained:

<Prompt description="Give me total revenue and total payments for March and April. Compare the two months and explain the percentage difference." icon="user-robot-xmarks" actions={["copy"]}>
Give me total revenue and total payments for March and April. Compare the two months and explain the percentage difference.
</Prompt>

## Rates with an unclear denominator

<Prompt description="What percentage of submissions failed?" icon="user-robot-xmarks" actions={["copy"]}>
What percentage of submissions failed?
</Prompt>

Failed out of *what*? All submissions ever? First attempts only? Every rate needs its numerator and denominator spelled out, plus a timeframe:

<Prompt description="Out of all submissions in the last 90 days, what percentage failed on the first attempt? Give me total submissions and total failed submissions too." icon="user-robot-xmarks" actions={["copy"]}>
Out of all submissions in the last 90 days, what percentage failed on the first attempt? Give me total submissions and total failed submissions too.
</Prompt>

The same applies to resolution rates, conversion rates, and anything else expressed as a percentage. If timing matters, say which date anchors the calculation — *"use the issue creation date as the reference point"* removes a whole class of ambiguity.

## "Why" questions with no breakdown

<Prompt description="Why is the outstanding balance high?" icon="user-robot-xmarks" actions={["copy"]}>
Why is the outstanding balance high?
</Prompt>

"High" is subjective, and a why-question with no structure invites a subjective answer. Turn it into a measurement with a breakdown, and the "why" tends to fall out of the numbers:

<Prompt description="What's the total balance older than 90 days? Break it down by category and by account owner." icon="user-robot-xmarks" actions={["copy"]}>
What's the total balance older than 90 days? Break it down by category and by account owner.
</Prompt>

Contribution questions work the same way — instead of *"how much revenue came from customers?"*, ask for the amount **and** the percentage of the total it represents, for a named period. Without the reference total, a raw number floats free.

# A template, if you want one

<Prompt description="For [timeframe], calculate [metric]. Include [the supporting totals], broken down by [dimension] if needed." icon="user-robot-xmarks" actions={["copy"]}>
For [timeframe], calculate [metric]. Include [the supporting totals], broken down by [dimension] if needed.
</Prompt>

Fill in the brackets and delete what you don't need. One metric per prompt — if you want five KPIs, five short prompts beat one long one.
